The strategy in one paragraph

The Short-Term-Holder (STH) cost basis is the average price paid for every bitcoin that moved on-chain in the last 155 days — the break-even line of the market's most price-sensitive participants. Bull markets hold above it, bear markets live below it, and capitulation happens 25–35 % beneath it. The strategy buys everything at 75 % of the STH cost basis (or at the Long-Term-Holder PVR −0.5σ band), rides established up-trends from 12 % above the line, and sells in four slices as price climbs through the LTH PVR +1σ, +1.5σ, +2σ and +2.5σ bands — each slice only once short-term momentum has turned negative — buying the slices back after a 10 % pull-back (or when price returns to within 10 % of the cost basis). Version 2.1 changes only the slice sizes: an exhaustive search of every ladder showed that selling most of the position at the first gated rung (keeping 30 → 20 → 10 → 0 % instead of 75 → 50 → 25 → 0 %) and re-buying the pull-back is worth more than v2 — on identical trade dates, with the same drawdown. Idle cash earns stable-coin yield.

The scale-out ladder and the dip re-buy (v2) — slice sizes optimised in v2.1

SCALE OUT R15 · Ladder

  • Inside a hold, each time price closes above the next LTH PVR upper band the BTC share steps down: +1σ → 30 %, +1.5σ → 20 %, +2σ → 10 %, +2.5σ → 0 %
  • A hold entered above a rung starts there; nothing is sold on day one

GATE Momentum gate

  • A rung is armed when price crosses the band, but the slice is only sold once the 2-day rate of change is ≤ −2 % — the strategy sells the first sign of weakness at height, never into strength
  • Without this gate a ladder under-performs the single all-out — tested, not assumed

RE-BUY R16 · Dip re-buy

  • After a slice, go back to 100 % BTC when price is 10 % below its post-slice peak or back at ≤ 110 % of the STH cost basis
  • Re-arms the ladder for the next leg up; at most 3 re-buys per hold
  • 5-day cooldown between any slice and re-buy (either direction) — no next-day flip-flops. Full exits ignore the cooldown
  • Why sell so much at +1σ? The slice is sold on weakness and re-bought ~10 % lower — the more cash raised at the top of the swing, the more is re-bought on the dip; +2.5σ still catches the blow-off

Entry rules — when cash becomes bitcoin (unchanged from v1)

Exit rules — when bitcoin becomes cash

SELL R2 · Capitulation exit

  • From a capitulation hold, price recovers to 95 % of the cost basis; sell only once 2-day momentum ≤ −2 %
  • Fired: Jul-18 $8.2k · Jan-20 $8.1k · Aug-21 $45.4k · Apr-22 $46.4k · Nov-22 $19.4k · Oct-23 $26.8k · May-26 $80.5k

SELL R3 / R12 · Trend exit

  • R3 price ≤ 97 % of the cost basis, momentum ≤ −2 % and the cost basis flat or falling over 21 days · R12 trailing stop 35 % below the peak since entry
  • Fired: Sep-19 $8.1k (stop) · Mar-20 $7.95k · Sep-23 $25.8k · Aug-24 $59.9k · Mar-25 $88.9k · Oct-25 $109.5k

SELL R15 · Final rung (+2.5σ)

  • The last rung of the ladder is the v1 euphoria exit: price ≥ the LTH PVR +2.5σ band, momentum turned → sell everything. Replaces v1's "2× cost basis" rule
  • Fired: Dec-17 $16.6k · Apr-21 $61.4k · Oct-21 $61.1k
Filters — what stops bad trades

FILTER Minimum hold

  • No exit rule may fire within 15 days of an entry — removes same-week whipsaws (ladder slices are exempt)

FILTER Post-exit wait

  • After a final-rung exit, no trend re-entry until price has come back to 85 % of the cost basis (v1: 90 %) — sits out the top-formation chop

FILTER Macro top

  • No trend entries while price is above the LTH PVR +2.5σ band; capitulation buys are never blocked
Why these two on-chain lines

STH cost basis — the regime line

  • Average acquisition price of coins younger than 155 days (Research Bitcoin realized_price_sth); reacts within weeks and has separated bull from bear since 2012

LTH PVR bands — the cycle extremes

  • BitWealth's Long-Term-Holder Profit-to-Volatility model. −0.5σ = long-term holders near break-even (accumulate); +1σ…+2.5σ = statistically extreme unrealised profit (distribute in steps)
