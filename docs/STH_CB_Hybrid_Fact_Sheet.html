STH Cost Basis Hybrid Strategy

Cycle-aware Bitcoin allocation — all-in at capitulation, all-out at euphoria, driven by on-chain holder cost bases
Prepared by BitWealth · Confidential — For Review Only
The strategy in one paragraph

The Short-Term-Holder (STH) cost basis is the average price paid for every bitcoin that moved on-chain in the last 155 days — the break-even line of the market's most price-sensitive participants. Bull markets hold above it; bear markets live below it; capitulation happens 25–35 % beneath it. The strategy holds either 100 % BTC or 100 % cash and switches on a small set of rules built on that line: it buys everything when price collapses to 75 % of the STH cost basis (or to the Long-Term-Holder PVR −0.5σ band), sells everything when price returns to the cost basis after a capitulation, re-enters when a new up-trend establishes itself 12 % above the line, exits a trend when it fails back through the line or falls 35 % from its peak, and distributes at euphoria — the LTH PVR +2.5σ band or twice the STH cost basis. Idle cash earns stable-coin yield. Every sell is momentum-gated so the strategy does not sell into strength.

A capitulation hold is upgraded to a trend hold if price rallies straight through the cost basis to +12 % — no round-trip sale. Euphoria distribution (below) applies to both hold states.

Entry rules — when cash becomes bitcoin

BUY R1 · Capitulation

  • Price closes at or below 75 % of the STH cost basis
  • …and the 2-day rate of change is ≥ 0 — the knife has stopped falling
  • Deploy 100 % of cash
  • Fired: Apr-18 $7.0k · Mar-20 $5.3k · Jun-21 $33.9k · Jan-22 $37.5k · Jun-22 $21.0k · Feb-26 $69.4k

BUY R14 · LTH −0.5σ

  • Price closes at or below the Long-Term-Holder PVR −0.5σ band (BitWealth's LTH PVR model)
  • Catches bottoms where STH capitulation is shallow but long-term holders are under water
  • Fired: Nov-18 $5.2k · Nov-19 $7.3k · Nov-22 $16.7k · Jun-26 $59.9k

BUY R3 / R5 / R8 · Trend

  • R3 fresh trend: price ≥ 112 % of the cost basis
  • R5 after a trend exit, re-enter at 102 % (the ladder)
  • R8 or as soon as 2-day momentum ≥ +10 % with price above the line
  • Fired: Aug-21 $48.8k · Sep-24 $63.7k · May-25 $97.0k
Exit rules — when bitcoin becomes cash

SELL R2 · Capitulation exit

  • From a capitulation hold, price recovers to 95 % of the cost basis
  • Sell only once 2-day momentum ≤ −2 % — never into strength
  • Fired: Jul-18 $8.2k · Jan-20 $8.1k · Aug-21 $45.4k · Apr-22 $46.4k · Nov-22 $19.4k · Oct-23 $26.8k · May-26 $80.5k

SELL R3 / R12 · Trend exit

  • R3 price ≤ 97 % of the cost basis, momentum ≤ −2 % and the cost basis itself flat or falling over 21 days
  • R12 trailing stop: price 35 % below its peak since entry
  • Fired: Sep-19 $8.1k (stop) · Mar-20 $7.95k · Sep-23 $25.8k · Aug-24 $59.9k · Mar-25 $88.9k · Oct-25 $109.5k

SELL R4 / R6 · Euphoria

  • R4 price ≥ the LTH PVR +2.5σ band — historically the final blow-off zone
  • R6 or price ≥ 2× the STH cost basis
  • Sell everything regardless of momentum
  • Fired: Dec-17 $15.2k · Mar-21 $57.1k · Oct-21 $61.0k
Filters — what stops bad trades

FILTER Minimum hold

  • No exit rule may fire within 15 days of an entry — removes same-week whipsaws

FILTER Post-distribution wait

  • After a euphoria sale, no trend re-entry until price has come back to 90 % of the cost basis — the strategy sits out the top-formation chop

FILTER Macro top

  • No trend entries while price is above the LTH PVR +2.5σ band; capitulation buys are never blocked
Why these two on-chain lines

STH cost basis — the regime line

  • Average acquisition price of coins younger than 155 days (Research Bitcoin realized_price_sth)
  • Reacts within weeks; separates bull from bear with remarkable consistency since 2012
  • Deep discounts to it (≤ 75 %) have marked every major capitulation: 2018, Mar-2020, mid-2021, 2022

LTH PVR bands — the cycle extremes

  • BitWealth's Long-Term-Holder Profit-to-Volatility model (used by the LTH PVR DCA product)
  • +2.5σ = long-term holders sitting on statistically extreme unrealised profit → distribution
  • −0.5σ = long-term holders near break-even → generational accumulation
