1 · On-Chain Regime Signal

The signal layer

The Short-Term-Holder (STH) cost basis is the average price paid for every bitcoin that changed hands in roughly the last five months — the break-even line of the market's most price-sensitive participants. Bull markets hold above it, bear markets live below it, and capitulation happens well beneath it.

Because it is computed from blockchain transaction data rather than from price patterns, it reflects real capital at risk and reacts within weeks, not months. A second on-chain series — the profit position of long-term holders — marks the cycle extremes where distribution begins.

Why it matters
  • Separates the bull and bear regimes cleanly since 2012
  • Responds fast enough to catch capitulation lows within days
  • Independent of price-only technicals and sentiment
  • One decision per day — low frequency, low noise, low churn

2 · Decisive Allocation

The position engine

Rather than drip-feeding a fixed amount regardless of conditions, the strategy is binary by design: it deploys all available cash when price collapses to a capitulation discount against the cost basis, or when an up-trend is confirmed above it — and moves back to cash when the trend breaks.

Monthly contributions follow the same logic: they are invested immediately while the strategy is in the market and held as cash (earning yield) while it is not. Over the back-test the strategy was in Bitcoin for roughly three-quarters of all days.

Advantages
  • Full exposure in the regimes that produce the returns
  • Sidesteps the deepest part of each bear market
  • Cash on hand exactly when capitulation buys appear
  • No lump-sum timing risk — entries are rule-driven, not date-driven

3 · Scale-Out & Re-Buy

The distribution engine

Cycle tops are processes, not moments. As long-term holders' unrealised profit climbs through progressively more extreme levels, the strategy steps its Bitcoin share down in slices — but each slice is only sold once short-term momentum has turned, never into strength.

After a meaningful pull-back the position is rebuilt, re-arming the ladder for the next leg. A no-loss rule ensures a slice is never sold below the price it was last bought at, so the ladder cannot churn capital away in a sideways market.

Advantages
  • Never all-in at a cycle top — profit is banked in stages
  • Raises cash on weakness and redeploys it lower
  • Final rung exits completely at the blow-off extreme
  • Tested against single all-out exits and un-gated ladders — the gated ladder wins

4 · Idle-Cash Yield with USDPC

The cash-efficiency layer

Roughly a quarter of the time the strategy is deliberately out of Bitcoin. Instead of idling, that cash is swept into a yield-bearing stable-coin and converted back just-in-time when the next buy signal fires. The yield is modest, but it compounds across every cash period — and it is earned precisely in the bear-market stretches when Bitcoin is falling.

In the trailing five-year back-test below, idle-cash yield is assumed at ~10% APY with a small conversion fee on each sweep. You can switch it off in the back-tester to see the strategy on its own.

Advantages
  • Cash earns while waiting — no “dead” capital
  • Conversion is automated and just-in-time; no manual steps
  • Yield accrues exactly when the strategy is de-risked
  • Fully transparent: every sweep and conversion fee is on your statement

Stable-coin yields are variable and not guaranteed; the back-test assumption is illustrative. Stable-coins carry issuer and smart-contract risk.

What this strategy is not

  • Not a guarantee. Every parameter was optimised on history. Fitted on one half of history and tested on the other, the strategy beat holding by roughly 1.5–2× — not the 50× of the full-history back-test. Plan on the smaller number.
